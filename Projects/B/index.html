Project 11

Limitations

Casino advertising in Belarus is constrained by current legislation, proposed 2026 restrictions, and operational boundaries.

LI-1

Current statutory content rules. Under Article 23 of Law No. 225-Z, gambling ads must not create the impression that winning is guaranteed or that gambling is a way to earn income, must not exaggerate the chance of winning, and must not use images of minors. Gambling ads are banned on TV and radio from 07:00 to 21:00, in buildings of educational, healthcare and cultural institutions, and in media intended for people under 18 or specialising in ecology, education, or health.

LI-2

Proposed 2026 restrictions (MART draft). The draft law prepared by MART and sent for interagency approval proposes: a ban on online advertising of lotteries, gambling, electronic interactive games and gambling establishments (with some exceptions); a ban on such ads in ground public transport; additional time limits on TV and radio; a ban on images of people and animals; and a ban on mentioning betting bonuses.

LI-3

Audience & financial access. Presidential Decree No. 64 of 20 February 2026 prohibits people under 18 and people restricted from gambling (including self-exclusion) from taking part in electronic instant lotteries. Mandatory identity verification (Decree No. 226) and bank daily card limits restrict the conversion of high-spending players from ad campaigns.

LI-4

Platform & permit barriers. Global ad platforms enforce strict compliance gates: Google shows gambling ads in Belarus only for MNS-licensed operators with Google certification. Outdoor billboards cannot be placed without a permit from the local executive committee.

LI-5

Reputational & social constraints. Growing public and state concern over gambling addiction creates high reputational risk. Aggressive ad saturation, such as casino billboards next to public spaces, causes public pushback and speeds up regulatory restrictions.

LI-6

Analytical scope. MART's 2026 draft law is still under review; its final text and the full list of exceptions for online advertising have not been published, so long-term media planning faces high uncertainty.
